## 1. What changed and why

The push and the extension clip existed only inside `scripts/eval_told_clip.py`,
a harness. Three consumers — the evaluation suite, the web export and
`predict.py` — each re-derived Stage 2 with their own `np.clip` call, which is
the shape of duplication that lets a "$39.68M for Marcus Smart" survive review in
one path while another path is correct. This branch moves the whole post-Stage-1
chain into **`src/model/stages.py`** and points all three consumers at it. The
chain is `latent → push → clip(lo, hi) → stage 3`, with the push in Stage 2
because it is the upward half of the same CBA bound, and `stage3(pred, *,
is_extension, ext_cap_pct)` in one function whose docstring carries the contract:
it only ever lowers, it is a no-op where `is_extension` is false or
`ext_cap_pct` is NaN, and it never reads the target. τ = 0.52 and margin = 1.05
are module constants marked pre-registered; neither was touched.

The suite now scores three arms off **one** fit pass — clip only (the v7.13x
champion), + push (ex ante), + push + Stage 3 (champion, told route) — so the
arms ride on identical fitted models and the convention change is legible in the
suite's own output rather than only in a RESULT. `oof_groupkfold` and
`rolling_forward` gained a multi-arm return; a fitter that returns one array
behaves exactly as before, so the six `scripts/eval_*.py` harnesses that call
them are untouched and uncompiled-against-nothing-new.

### Design call on the free-agent branch of `export_web`

`_add_free_agents` deliberately did not clip FA rows at all ("Value Board shows
market value; there is no CBA clip without a contract"). A push without its clip
can land a player above his legal max, so the ceiling has to come with it. I
measured before choosing: **zero of the 143 FA rows have a latent above their
computed ceiling**, so adding the ceiling is a no-op today and only bounds future
pushed rows. The **floor** is not applied to FA rows, and that is deliberate —
see the `_compute_floor` anomaly below.

## 5. Anomalies

**A. The C1 relative calibration gate fails (+0.0074 against a +0.005 bar).**
Slope 0.9809 → 0.9734. The source is the push, not Stage 3: the push alone takes
the slope to **0.9455** (growth +0.0354, a gross failure), and Stage 3 recovers
about four fifths of that. Mechanism, in one sentence: the push moves 33 rows
upward at the top of the range and, because a max row's observed pay is its
ceiling and the margin is 1.05, it systematically overshoots the few rows whose
ceiling is inflated above their tier — which tilts the regression of actual on
predicted. This is a real cost of the adopted change and the architect should
decide it explicitly rather than inherit it; it does not appear in the task
brief's table.

**B. The push's own ΔSel is negative (−0.0057, t = −1.63) and its 2025 forward
origin drops hard** (0.8173 → 0.7689, −0.048). Stage 3 restores it to 0.8166.
The adopted bundle is fine; the push *alone* is not, and it should not be shipped
without Stage 3 in front of it. Worth pinning in VERSION_HISTORY so nobody later
"simplifies" by dropping the clip.

**C. Stage 3 alone is +0.0143, above the +0.01 red-flag line.** Explained: 13
rows, mean |err| $8.44M → $0.42M, of which Marcus Smart 2022 alone is
$22.22M → $0.00M. Every moved row is a legal-bound correction and none regresses.
Not a leak — `ext_cap_pct` reads the CBA multiple, the player's *prior*-season
pay and the published Estimated Average Player Salary, never this row's target.
Hard gate re-checked on this branch: **0 rows paid above their own extension cap.**

**D. Stage 3 quietly does a second thing: on extension rows it caps at
`tier_ceiling_pct`, not at `max_eligible_pct`.** `attach_extension_cap` takes
`min(tier, legal)`, so where the 1.08× no-decrease floor has inflated
`max_eligible_pct` above the tier, Stage 3 removes that inflation. This is
defensible — the no-decrease rule legalises escalator pay, which an extension's
first year is not — but it is a second mechanism hiding inside one clip. It
affects **2 of 156** extension rows (Durant 2026: ceiling $63.03M vs tier
$57.74M; Turner 2023: $41.66M vs $40.81M) and **neither is one of the 13 moved
rows**, so it contributes nothing to the measured gain today.

**E. Rows where the two ceilings disagree, as asked.** 70 rows have a tier
ceiling more than $10M above their raise cap; the widest are Steven Adams 2025
and T.J. McConnell 2025 (both $54.13M vs $18.10M — the raise cap is the
Estimated-Average-Salary floor, not a multiple of their pay), Kelly Olynyk 2024
($49.21M vs $17.07M) and P.J. Washington 2026 ($49.49M vs $19.81M, the $29.7M
gap the extension-cap module documents). None of these is pushed, so the gap is
latent risk rather than realised error — it is exactly the population Stage 3
protects against if the classifier ever becomes more confident.

**F. Confirmation split.** The brief asked for a re-check now that the push is
included. Of the 52 rows where the push gate fires, **10 are confirmation rows**;
of the 33 whose prediction actually moves, **8 are confirmation rows**. Of the 13
Stage-3 rows, **0** are — the told-clip finding stands. Confirmation R² moves
0.8070 → 0.8305 and selection 0.7825 → 0.7928, i.e. the canary improves *more*
than the selection pool, which is the reassuring direction. The confirmation
split was not read by any decision here.

### #NN — `predict.py` fans out rows on duplicate names in `heights.csv`

**Symptom.** `predict(2026)` returns 458 rows for 456 players — two names fan
out. "Brandon Williams" appears twice with different heights (73 in and 78 in)
and therefore two *different* predictions ($9.20M and $10.15M); "Nate Williams"
appears twice with predictions that happen to agree, which is the more dangerous
case because nothing about the output looks wrong. `heights.csv` legitimately holds
several distinct BBRef players per normalised name (`willibr01` / `willibr03`,
three `Charles Jones`, three `Charles Smith`, …), and `predict.py`'s
`merge(heights, on="player_name_norm", how="left")` fans the prediction row out
once per match.

**Reproduce.**
```
python -c "import sys;sys.path.insert(0,'.');from src.model.predict import predict;d=predict(2026);print(len(d), d.player_name_norm.duplicated().sum())"
```

**Fix.** The merge needs a key that survives the collision. Either
de-duplicate `heights.csv` by BBRef id against the player actually in
`impact_metrics.csv`, or make the merge `validate="m:1"` so it raises instead of
fanning out. Do not simply `drop_duplicates` — that silently assigns one
player's height to another. `export_web.py` is not affected (heights arrive
through the training table, and `_load_signing_types` already dedupes and
asserts the row count).

**Done when.** `predict(2026)` returns one row per player and
`player_name_norm.duplicated().sum() == 0` (it is 2 today).

### #NN — `_compute_floor` is frame-relative and silently wrong on a prediction frame

**Symptom.** `_compute_floor` derives `floor_pct` as the (season, experience)
median of the frame's OWN `is_at_floor` rows, and `is_at_floor` reads `cap_pct`.
On a frame whose `cap_pct` has been set to the model's own latent — which is what
`predict.py` does before calling it, and what any FA-only frame would do — the
lookup is circular. On the 143-row 2026 free-agent frame it returns a **negative
floor** (Bismack Biyombo, −$1.21M) and lifts 15 fringe free agents to a
"minimum" that came out of the model's own predictions.

**Reproduce.** Build the FA frame `export_web._add_free_agents` builds, set
`cap_pct = latent`, call `_compute_floor`, and print `floor_pct.min()`.

**Fix.** The floor is a CBA scale, not an empirical quantity: derive it once
from the full training frame (or a curated minimum-scale table) and look it up by
(season, experience bucket) for any prediction frame. The docstring already says
"at inference the floor clip needs only season + experience" — the code does not
yet honour it.

**Interim guard shipped on `worker/wire-stage3`**: `export_web._add_free_agents`
does not call `_compute_floor` and does not floor FA rows, with the reason in a
comment. `predict.py` still does, unchanged — fixing it there moves numbers and
is outside this brief.

**Done when.** `floor_pct` for a given (season, experience bucket) is the same
number whether computed on the training frame, the FA frame or a single row, and
is never negative.
